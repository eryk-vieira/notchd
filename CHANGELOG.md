# Changelog

All user-visible changes. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). The version is `manifest.json`'s. Released sections are never edited.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Navigating the notches from the keyboard: `navigate` over IPC (bind it to a key, e.g. SUPER+N) highlights every notch, hidden ones included, with a small label at its top right: its number and name. `1`–`9` or the arrows (`h`/`l`) select one, which opens as a preview while the others show closed; `Tab` walks its tabs, `Enter` pins it open (the other notches close) and `Esc`, Ctrl+Esc or the shortcut again leaves everything as it was. Keys also act on the selected notch: `s` opens its settings, `x` twice removes it, and `m` moves it (arrows for the edge, Shift+arrows along it, Tab for the monitor; Enter keeps it, Esc puts it back).
- Any notch pinned open (a click, a shortcut, navigation) takes the keyboard, not only an agent's: Escape or Ctrl+Escape closes it, and a focus or workspace shortcut that moves to a window closes it too. Never on hover alone. A notch opened on the monitor without focus no longer closes at once: it keeps the keyboard until Escape.
- Plan usage for `claude` and `codex`: the open agent shows a bar per limit (5 hours, the week, a model's week) with how much is used and when it resets, and peeks once when one passes 80 % (`usageWarn`) and when it is reached. notchd asks the endpoint each CLI calls, every 5 minutes and when the agent finishes a task, with the login the CLI keeps, which it only reads; when that lapses, the last figures stay, marked stale, until the agent renews it. Settings: Modules › Agents › its gear › Usage.
- Agents, a kind of module of their own: `claude` and `codex` are listed apart on the Modules page, under Agents, and share one set of fields (folder, command, stop when idle, size, font), which a new agent gets by saying `"agent": { "command": ... }` in its JSON file. `agent <id> toggle` opens or closes the notch that shows an agent, on its tab, whatever the notch is called; bind that instead of `toggleNotch <name>`, which renaming the notch breaks. `agent <id> new` and `agent <id> "ask ..."` do what `claude new` and `claude "ask ..."` do, which stay.
- An agent without a folder of its own starts in notchd's: `notchd` in your Documents folder, made the first time, instead of your home.
- The README image shows the agents: Claude and Codex in the closed notch, a plan-usage warning, a script's fine-tune, and Claude open in its terminal with its limits; the OSD left it.
- Leaving the notch from the keyboard: Ctrl+Escape in the `claude` or `codex` terminal closes the notch (the agent goes on), and a focus or workspace shortcut that moves to a window closes it too. Before, the open notch kept the keyboard until a click away.
- Tabs (`tabs`, on by default; settings: Notches › its gear › Layout › Tabs): a notch with several modules shows one at a time open, with a tab for each, instead of all of them one above the other. Opening the notch from a module (a shortcut, `claude "ask ..."`) picks its tab. Closed, it shows every active module side by side, not just the highest-priority one. `tabs: false` brings back the old way.
- Icons for idle modules (`idleIcons`, on by default; settings: Notches › its gear › Layout): a module that is active with nothing to show closed, like a timer that isn't running, shows its icon in the closed notch. Modules declare it with `icon`.
- News you missed while the notch was open: an agent that finishes while you look at another tab gets a dot on its tab and on the closed notch (until you open it), and its peek shows as the notch closes. Modules set `attention` for it.
- `claude` and `codex` modules: Claude Code and Codex in their own terminal UIs inside the notch, in a terminal from the optional `qmltermwidget` package (`make restart` installs it with pacman; without it, the notch says what to install). The closed notch shows the session's title, which the agent makes after the first message, read from the title it gives the terminal, and a green dot while it works; open, the terminal takes the keyboard, Escape included, and closes from its × button or the shortcut. The agent starts the first time the notch opens and keeps running while it is closed, peeking when it finishes something; the restart button, or `claude new` / `codex new` over IPC, starts a new one, and `claude "ask <question>"` opens it and types the question in. The terminal takes the theme's background and foreground. Settings: Modules › Agents › Claude, › Codex (folder, command, width, height, font, stop when idle). Not loaded by default: give each a notch of its own and bind `agent <id> toggle` to a key.
- Modules you type into: the notch takes the keyboard while it is pinned open, by a click, a shortcut (`toggleNotch`) or a press on the module's field (`requestKeyboard()`), never on hover alone; Escape closes it. Modules can also set their expanded view's width (`expandedWidth`), and open or close their notch (`openNotch()`, `closeNotch()`).
- An app icon: the notch with an orange dot and an `n`, in Gruvbox colors. `assets/icons/` has the SVG, PNGs at every size from 16 to 1024 px, a `.ico` and a one-color symbolic SVG.
- Omarchy: a notchd button in the bar, with the app icon. Its panel lists every notch, where it is and what it shows; clicking one opens or closes it, its gear opens that notch's settings, and Settings (or a right click on the icon) opens the settings window.
- `timer` module: a countdown or a pomodoro, started from the expanded notch or with `timer 25m`, `timer pomodoro`, `timer toggle`, `timer +5m`, `timer stop` over IPC. The closed notch shows the time left; at the end it peeks, even over the open notch, and sends a desktop notification. Its lengths, rounds, auto-start and notification are in settings (Modules › Timer). Not loaded by default.
- `media.equalizer` (settings: Modules › Now playing › Equalizer): `"smooth"` (the default) glides the bars at 60 fps, `"light"` makes them jump about six times a second, for less CPU.
- `resources` module: CPU, memory and the disk holding `/`, each with a bar and how much is used in the expanded notch, and their percentages in the closed notch. One that runs short (90 % or more; the CPU for about 10 s in a row, so a burst doesn't count) turns red and peeks once. `resources.compact: "short"` (settings: Modules › Resources › Closed notch) keeps them out of the closed notch until one runs short. Not loaded by default.
- Modules can have settings of their own, declared with their title in a JSON file next to the module (`media.json`) and read with `setting(key)`: stored under the module's id, shown on the module's own page, loaded or not.
- Notches never sit on each other: on the same edge of a monitor they go side by side, each as near its `offset` as the others let it, and make room when one grows. An open notch grows over its neighbors, which hide until it closes.
- Standalone modules (`"standalone": true` in the module's JSON file): a fixed job and a place of their own. No notch shows one, even one whose `modules` list it. It loads unless `<id>.enabled` is `false`, peeks on the monitor you work on where `<id>.position` (`edge`, `offset`) says, top center by default, and draws over any notch there, which hides until it goes (settings: Modules › its gear). The Omarchy OSD is one, so `osd` is no longer in the default `modules`.
- Modules can set `peekOnFocusedScreen`: their peek shows once, on the focused monitor only, in the first notch there that shows the module, else at the top. For feedback on what you just did there.
- Settings: the Notches page lists the notches, where each is and what it shows; a gear opens a notch's page with everything it has of its own (name, modules, compact style, position, workspaces), and Add opens the new one's. A Modules page lists every module, notch modules and standalone ones apart, with where each shows (or whether a standalone one is on) and a gear to its own settings, even while no notch shows it. General comes first and has what applies to every notch.
- Several notches (`notches`; settings: the Notches page, a gear per notch): each with its own modules, compact style, position, workspaces and reserved space, inheriting the top-level keys it leaves out. A module may show in several. IPC gets `openNotch`, `closeNotch` and `toggleNotch` by notch id, and `state` lists the notches and their copies.
- `position.screen: "all"` (settings: Notches › Position › Screen › Every screen): a copy of the notch on every monitor, each hovered, pinned and dragged on its own. A peek shows on the focused monitor only, so changing the volume on one monitor no longer takes the notch away from the music playing on another.

- Drag the notch onto another monitor: a preview shows it there, and the drop makes that monitor its home (`position.screen`; settings: Notches › Position › Screen), where the notch stays whichever monitor has focus, peeks included; a peek also shows at the top of the monitor you work on when no notch there shows it. Dropped on a workspace left out of `workspaces`, it adds that workspace, so the notch stays where it landed. Reserved space is kept on the home monitor only.
- Settings window with a sidebar of pages (General, Notches, Modules, Appearance), fields grouped by section, the edge picked from picture cards, segmented choices and switches for modules.
- Settings fields can be `boolean` or `note`, live on a `page`, and show a choice as picture cards (`display: "cards"`); hosts can add pages with `hostSettingsPages`.
- Theme tokens derived from the host's colors (`muted`, `faint`, `line`, `hover`, `selected`, `surface`, `danger`, `success`, radii). The Omarchy host takes `success` from the theme's `green` in `colors.toml`.
- `make check`: Node tests, QML behaviour tests, qmllint, plugin validation and offscreen visual scenarios.
- `reserveSpace` (settings: Notches › Layout › Keep windows clear): windows tile below the closed notch instead of under it. The space follows the closed notch, a sliver at rest and a compact notch's height while a module is active; peeks never move windows.
- `workspaces` (settings: Notches › Layout › Workspaces): the workspaces the closed notch shows on. On others it moves to a screen that shows one, or hides; peeks and the open notch still show, and reserved space is only kept where the notch can hang. Hyprland only.
- `position` (settings: Notches › Position): the hanging notch on any screen edge (`edge`: top, bottom, left, right) and anywhere along it (`offset`). On a side edge peeks and the expanded notch keep their layout and the notch grows sideways; reserved space moves to that edge.
- Hold the hanging notch for a moment and drag it to any edge (`draggable`, on by default; settings: Notches › Position › Drag to move); a quick click only pins it. It follows the nearest edge as you drag, snaps to the middle, and the drop is saved as `position`. Windows move once, when it lands. `recenter` over IPC puts it back at the top.
- Modules can give `verticalCompact` and `verticalPeek` views for a side edge; a module without a vertical compact view shows a dot there. The media and activities modules stack their compact view down the edge, and the Omarchy OSD stands its bar up.
- Settings fields can be a `number` (a slider with a fractional step, shown as a percentage with `display: "percent"`); card pictures get their option's `value`.
- README image of the notch in its four states, rendered from the real QML with `make hero`, in Omarchy's Gruvbox theme and wallpaper.

### Changed

- The expanded notch has no gear any more. In Omarchy the settings open from the notchd button in the bar; anywhere, with the `settings` IPC method.
- At either end of its edge the notch sits flush in the corner, square against the screen's side (or the bar), instead of short of it by its shoulder.
- Notches that come within a few pixels of each other on one edge (or are dropped near one) join flush into one shape, as deep as the deepest of them. One that opens grows from where it is and pushes the others aside, evenly in the middle of the edge and away from the screen's side against it, instead of hiding them.

- Modules read `notch.expanded` and `notch.peeking(module)` instead of `notch.mode` and `notch.peekModule`, since a module can show in several notches. Hosts read the place and workspaces from each notch (`service.notches`); `NotchSurface` no longer takes a `compositor` of its own.

- The repository root is the Omarchy plugin (`manifest.json` at the root). Point `~/.config/omarchy/plugins/eryk.notchd` at the checkout instead of `hosts/omarchy`.
- The standalone host is `shell.qml` at the repository root: run `qs -p <checkout>` instead of `qs -p hosts/standalone`.
- Documentation split into README (users), `docs/` (reference), DEVELOPMENT.md and CLAUDE.md.
- Hosts tell the service which edge their bar is on (`NotchService.barEdge`) instead of `NotchSurface.barOnTop`. The Omarchy host follows the bar's position, so with a bottom or side bar the notch drops out of it.
- Picking a field's default in the settings window removes the key from the config instead of writing the default.
- The compact media view shows the track's title and artist between the artwork and the equalizer, cut short past 240 design px; the artist drops out when there is no room for it.
- The compact notch is shorter (28 design px instead of 36), close to a bar's height: tighter padding, smaller corners, and smaller media artwork and activity icon.

### Fixed

- `claude` and `codex` down a side edge: the working dot sits on the mark's corner, instead of keeping an empty place under every mark while it is off.
- An open notch taller than the screen was cut off at the bottom, with no way to reach the rest: two terminals on a laptop screen, say. Past the room the screen has, its modules now scroll, with the wheel and a scrollbar.
- Switching a monitor off and on again no longer leaves its notches gone: they move to another monitor while it is off and come back to it, with the space they reserve.
- About 1300 binding-loop warnings in the log at start (and wasted work) with notches on a top or bottom edge and a side edge of one screen: the corners' insets read every notch's place, the side ones' included, which come from those insets.
- A notch in `notches` dragged to the top edge (or to the middle, or off its home screen) jumped back: the drop left out what equals the default, and the notch took it from the top-level `position` instead. The drop now writes whatever differs from what the notch inherits.
- Media artwork took a texture atlas the size of the monitor (about 25 MB) for an 80 px cover; it now gets a texture of its own, and scales down smoother.
- The default notch kept a window covering each monitor even while it showed nothing, about 25 MB of buffers; its windows now exist only while it shows a peek, and for 5 s after. A peek that needs it again after that shows about 50 ms later.
- A running timer redrew the notch four times a second; it now wakes as its clock turns, once a second, so the open notch's progress bar steps once a second too.
- Music playing cost a quarter to a third of a CPU core (on a 160 Hz monitor) and kept the compositor busy: the hidden default notch drew the media view on every monitor, and the equalizer and the progress bar never stopped easing. It now costs about 1 %: hidden copies hold no view, the equalizer and the progress bar move at 60 fps instead of the monitor's rate (8 % of a core while music plays, under 1 % of a 20-thread CPU), and a notch hidden over a fullscreen app stops its equalizer.
- The notch's animations looked choppy at their slow end: one side of the outline moved a whole pixel at a time while the other glided. It now moves in fractions of a pixel and settles on whole pixels, with its content kept on whole pixels throughout.
- Moving the pointer across the open notch's controls (the media buttons) closed and reopened it: those controls took the hover from the notch.
- With `reserveSpace`, windows took 4 s to reclaim the space after the last module ended; they now do as the notch finishes collapsing (400 ms).
- Media artwork, and its placeholder, vanished entirely under Qt's software renderer; it now shows with square corners there.

### Removed

- The compact view in the bar (`compactStyle: "bar"`, which was the default): every notch hangs from its edge. A config that still sets `compactStyle` changes nothing, so a notch that was in the bar now hangs from the top, in the middle; drag it or set `position` to move it. Modules lose `barView`, and the service `barStyleAvailable` and `barNotch`.
- `scripts/build-omarchy.sh` and `dist/`: no build step is needed any more.

Earlier history is in `git log`.
